Speed of execution is not quality of judgment.

AI has compressed the time between idea and output. Useful, except that slowness used to do something. Writing a document from scratch was annoying — and it was also where you figured out whether the document needed to exist.

Remove the friction, and you remove the pause that used to force thinking. Now you ship faster into the wrong direction.

I audit my notes every week — raw to processing, processing to refined. Most don't move. That friction is the filter. An idea that can't survive the audit didn't deserve to become an output.

The people who win with AI aren't the ones using it to go faster. They're the ones using it to go faster on the *right things* — which requires the slow, uncomfortable work of figuring out what those things are first.

Strategy doesn't compress. Taste doesn't auto-complete. Judgment isn't a prompt.

The bottleneck was never execution. It was always clarity. AI just makes that more obvious, faster.
